A look at every screen

Hand it over.
Keep your work.

Four screens, and only one of them is your child’s. Here is each, at a size you can actually read.

The play zone

What your child sees, every time. It fills the display and there is no way out of it.

  1. 1No address bar, and nothing to type into
  2. 2The only way to the grown-up screen
  3. 3Only the sites you added, as big soft buttons

  4. No new tab, no back button out of it, and no link that leads anywhere you did not allow. A child who taps everything finds only these tiles.

The grown-up screen

Everything you decide, in one place your child cannot open.

  1. 1Play time, apps, catalogue, settings
  2. 2Hide an app without deleting it
  3. 3Sites whose terms ask us not to filter say so
  4. 4Quitting is here, not hidden in a menu

  5. Getting in means holding a button for three seconds — or answering a small sum, if your child ever works the hold out.

Little fingers tap.
Grown-ups hold.

A quick tap does nothing at all — which is every tap a small child makes. Three full seconds of steady pressure is a different motion, and it is the one thing standing between your child and the rest of your laptop.

Prefer a sum? Switch it in settings and the hold stops working entirely.

Try it — press and keep pressing.

When play time is up

It ends by itself, so it was never your decision in the moment.

  1. 1Your child can ask; only you can answer
  2. 2And a way out that is always there

  3. Set the session length once and it starts again every time the app opens. The countdown only appears in the last thirty seconds, so there is nothing to watch or bargain with. Grant fifteen minutes, an hour or the rest of the day when it runs out — your usual limit does not move.

Adding a site

Paste an address. Sukhi Play works out the rest and shows you before it saves.

  1. 1Pick a shape and a colour — never a favicon
  2. 2The address, and the hosts it needs, found for you
  3. 3Advert filtering, on unless you say otherwise

  4. Or start from the catalogue: sites already set up, and three apps that come with the download and need no internet at all.

And the rest

  • No favicons, no logosEvery picture on a tile is a shape and a colour we drew. Nothing is fetched from the sites you add, so nothing on your child’s screen is theirs to change.
  • Keyboard shortcuts held downAlt+Tab, Alt+F4, Command+Q, Print Screen and the rest are caught while the window has focus. The Windows key is the one exception, and we say so on the Windows page rather than hoping you do not notice.
  • No downloads, no popups, no new windowsA site cannot open a window, start a download, or ask for your camera. The one thing that may write a file is a bundled app saving a picture your child drew.
  • Nothing is sent anywhereNo sign-in, no analytics, no telemetry, no crash reporting. The only request Sukhi Play makes on its own is checking for a newer version, and only when you press the button.
  • Free, and open sourceMIT licensed. Every line is public, including the parts that decide what your child can reach.
