Two screens, really: the one your child gets, and the one only you can reach. Here is everything on both.

01

What your child gets

A screen with no way out of it, and nothing on it you did not put there.

Only what you chose

Full screen, no address bar, no new tab, no back button out of it. A child who taps everything finds only these tiles.

Adverts and trackers, refused

Blocked before they load, on every site you add.

Apps that need no internet

A colouring book, a keyboard playground and first games, inside the download.

0accounts, trackers, reports

Nothing is sent anywhere

No sign-in, no analytics, nothing phoning home.

Then it stops by itself

No argument, because it was never your decision in the moment. The countdown only appears in the last thirty seconds, so there is nothing to watch or bargain with.

02

What only you can reach

Behind a gate a three-year-old does not get through.

The grown-up screen

Play time, the apps, the catalogue and every setting, in one place your child cannot open.

Hold it for three seconds

Hold

Quick taps do nothing at all. If your child ever works the hold out, swap it for a small sum instead.

20minutes, every time it opens

Set the session once

A standing rule, not a decision you make again each time. When it runs out you can grant fifteen minutes, an hour, or the rest of the day — and your usual limit stays where it is.

Add any site by its address

Paste an address. Sukhi Play loads it, works out which hosts it needs, and suggests a name and a picture — change any of it before you save. Or start from the catalogue.

And the rest

  • No favicons, no logosEvery tile is a shape and a colour we drew. Nothing is fetched from the sites you add.
  • Keyboard shortcuts heldAlt+Tab, Alt+F4, Command+Q, Print Screen. The Windows key is the one exception, and we say so.
  • No downloadsA site cannot write a file. The one thing that can is a bundled app saving your child’s picture.
  • It sets itself upThe first run picks a few sites with you and asks how you want to get back in.
  • Free and open sourceMIT licensed. Every line is public, including the parts that decide what your child can reach.
  • Works on your machinemacOS, Windows and Linux. No account, no payment, no trial.
