Sukhi Colouring

Pick a picture and colour it in, or start on a blank page. Forty outlines, three pens that behave like different materials, and a shelf that keeps what they made so they can carry on tomorrow.

Open it in your browser It is part of the playground →

Free · no account · nothing collected · works with no internet

A page being coloured in

One picture, as big as the page will take. Colours down the left where a hand rests, everything else down the right.

Forty pictures to start from

Animals, things that go, the garden, the sky. Or close it and draw on the blank page instead.

A shelf that keeps their work

Not a picture of the page: the page itself. Open one tomorrow and carry on from where they left it.

What is in it

Three pens that feel different

A pencil that feathers, a crayon that skips and leaves the paper showing through, and a rainbow that changes colour as it travels. Each one sounds different too.

Fill, for the parts that should be solid

Tap inside a shape and it fills to the lines. A two-year-old who cannot yet stay inside them still gets a coloured-in cat.

A butterfly that mirrors

Turn it on and everything drawn on one side appears on the other. It is the single most popular thing in the app with the child it was built for.

Twenty colours and three widths

Enough to choose from, few enough to see all at once. No colour picker, no sliders, no menus.

Back, forward, and start again

Nothing a small hand does is permanent. Start again asks first, and clears the page behind a fade so it is something you watch rather than something you find.

English and Korean

Both are complete, including the picture names. The language lives behind the settings cog, not on the main screen where a child would press it.

It is the same app on a phone

Not a cut-down version: the same pictures, the same pens, the same shelf. The rails fold into strips along the top and bottom, the page takes everything in between, and every control stays a size a small hand can hit. It works down to 320 px.

A parent can keep it on the home screen, where it opens full screen with no address bar for a small finger to land in, and keeps working with no signal. The playground page has the three lines of instructions for each platform.

What it does not do

There is no account and nothing to sign in to. Nothing a child makes leaves their own machine: the shelf is stored in the browser, and there is no server on the other end of it to store anything anywhere else. There is no advertising, no analytics inside the app, no links out, and no way to wander from this page to any other.

It also ships inside Sukhi Play itself, where it sits behind a gate a child cannot open, alongside everything else you chose for them.

Where it came from

Sukhi Colouring is a derived work. The idea, the shape of it and the sound it makes come from Kids Coloring by oh-namgyu, MIT licensed, which Sukhi Play bundled for its first two years. None of the code does — this was written from nothing, which is what made it possible to add a blank page, a shelf, a second language and a layout that works on a phone.

It is the first app built for Sukhi Play rather than allowed into it. More are coming.
