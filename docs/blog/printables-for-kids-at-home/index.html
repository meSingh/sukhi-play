Printable name labels, stickers and bookmarks for children

Jazz's Studio is a free stationery maker for children of seven and up: name labels, stickers, bookmarks, gift tags and door signs, each on one A4 page.

My daughter Jazz is ten and loves stationery, so I made her a studio where she designs her own name labels, stickers, bookmarks, gift tags and door signs, and prints them at home. It is called Jazz’s Studio, it is free, it runs in any browser at sukhiplay.com/playground/studio with no account, and every sheet prints on one A4 page from an ordinary home printer.

Your child can use all of it, with their own name on everything and, if they like, their own character in place of Jazz’s.

What can children make and print at home?

The Stationery part of the studio has five groups:

  • Stickers: “me stickers” of their character in different poses, or pattern stickers in circles, squares and hexagons to cut out.
  • Diary: a cover with their name and character on it, and pages with the date, a mood and lines to write on.
  • Labels and tags: name labels for books, boxes and pencil cases, and gift tags for presents.
  • Planners and lists: a week planner, and one to four to-do lists on a page with boxes to tick.
  • Bookmarks and signs: bookmarks, four to a page, and door signs that hang on a door handle.

Each sheet keeps its own colours, lettering and pattern, so changing the bookmark does not change the stickers. Beyond stationery there are wraps to turn a toilet roll or a cereal box into a pencil pot or desk tidy, a logo for each person with business cards and logo stickers, a name poster, secret messages in a real code, and a story page to write and draw. The studio page shows it all.

Who is it for?

Children of about seven and up. It was made for a ten-year-old, and most of it involves typing words and choosing between options, so it suits a child who reads. For younger children, Sukhi Colouring needs no reading at all.

How do I make name labels for school things?

  1. Open the studio and choose Stationery, then Labels and tags, then Name labels.
  2. Type the name. The line above it starts as “This belongs to”, and you can change it.
  3. Pick the colours and lettering, and whether their character goes on.
  4. Print, and cut the labels out.

A sheet can hold several people, each with their own name, which is handy with brothers and sisters.

Be realistic about where paper labels go. They are good on books, folders, pencil cases and boxes. They will not survive a washing machine or a dishwasher, so clothes and lunchboxes need proper labels made for that.

Do I need special sticker paper?

No. Plain printer paper works for everything: cut the shapes out and use a glue stick or clear tape. A strip of clear tape over a name label on a book cover also makes it last much longer.

If you want real stickers, the easy choice is whole-sheet sticker paper: one big sticky sheet that you print on and cut up yourself, so nothing has to line up with pre-cut shapes. Two things to check on the packet:

  • that it is made for your kind of printer, inkjet or laser
  • that your printer can take it (the printer’s manual lists the paper it accepts)

For bookmarks and door signs, thicker paper or thin card works best, if your printer will feed it. Covering a bookmark with clear tape on both sides makes a paper one survive a school bag.

How do I get it to print on one page?

Everything is drawn on A4 to the millimetre and prints on one page, and nothing sits closer than 10 mm to the edge, so printers that cannot print right to the edge are fine.

  • Box wraps from Make from a box should be printed at actual size (100%), not “fit to page”, so they fit the thing you measured. Each wrap has a line that should measure exactly 5 cm, so a ruler can check.
  • On an iPad, Safari adds the address and date at the top and bottom, and always prints a little smaller than actual size. That is fine for stickers and labels; for a wrap, measure the 5 cm line.
  • If your printer uses US Letter paper, look at the print preview before printing, because the sheets are drawn for A4.

Ideas for gift tags, bookmarks and door signs

  • Gift tags come eight to a sheet. Punch a hole and thread ribbon or string through. Leave the “To” and “From” empty and they can be written in by hand.
  • Bookmarks with their name, or a friend’s, down the middle make a good small present.
  • Door signs come two to a sheet, shaped to hang on a door handle, with their own words on them.
  • Kept prints mean next term’s labels are one press away. After printing, the studio offers to keep the print in My makes, and opening it later brings the sheet back exactly as it was.

Where are their designs kept?

On that device only. Nothing is uploaded and there is no account. Clearing the browser’s data for the site, or using a private window, clears it too.

To keep it on a phone or tablet, open it and use Add to Home Screen. Once it has been opened, it works without internet. Jazz’s Studio in the docs has the details.

Can I use it inside Sukhi Play?

Yes. It comes in the Sukhi Play download but switched off, so a younger child’s screen stays as it was. Open the grown-up screen, go to the catalogue and add Studio.

Inside Sukhi Play there is no print window, because a print window can save a file or open another program, both ways out of a locked screen. The page goes straight to the computer’s default printer, and the studio tells your child when it has gone, or to ask a grown-up if the printer did not answer.

Questions parents ask

Is Jazz's Studio free?

Yes. There is no account, no adverts and nothing to buy, and in a browser there is nothing to install either.

What age is it for?

About seven and up, because most of it means typing words and choosing between options. Younger children usually get more from Sukhi Colouring, which needs no reading at all.

Can my child put their own character on the stickers?

Yes. In Make it yours, Add a character takes a picture of a character on one plain colour, cuts the background out on the device and asks who it is. Nothing added to the studio leaves the device, though if you use Google Gemini to make a character from a photo, that photo goes to Google.

Why did my box wrap print the wrong size?

It was probably scaled to fit the page. Print it at actual size, or 100%, then measure the 5 cm line on the wrap with a ruler. An iPad always prints a little smaller than actual size.

Does it work on a phone or tablet?

Yes. Open it in the browser and use Add to Home Screen to keep it there; it works without internet once it has been opened. It prints from a phone or tablet too, on one A4 page.
