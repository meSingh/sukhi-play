How I set up Sukhi Play for my three-year-old

Start with two or three tiles, sit beside them for the first sessions, set a short play time and add sites slowly. The setup I would repeat, step by step.

Keep the first screen small: two or three tiles, a short play time, and you sitting beside them for the first few sessions. Show them the stop screen early so it becomes normal, change the way in if they learn it, and add sites one at a time as they grow. That is roughly how I set Sukhi Play up for my son, and what I would do again.

Sukhi was around three when I built it. He pressed every key and clicked every button, which on a normal laptop means adverts, popups, new tabs and eventually my work. Here is the setup, in the order I would do it.

What do I need before I start?

A Mac, a Windows PC or a Linux computer, and a few minutes. It is free, with no account and nothing to sign up for. The installers are on the download page.

The builds are signed but not notarised, so the first launch shows a warning on macOS and on Windows. Installing walks through getting past it, once per install.

How does the first run work?

The first time it opens, it asks who is using the computer. Choose I’m a grown-up and it walks you through four short steps:

  1. What your child will and will not be able to reach.
  2. Choose what they can open.
  3. How you get back out, with a chance to practise the three-second hold.
  4. Hand it over.

Do practise the hold. You want to know the feel of it before a small person is waiting. The first run has the detail.

How many sites should a three-year-old start with?

Two or three. More than that and a small child hops from tile to tile without settling on anything.

Three apps come switched on already, and for a three-year-old they are a reasonable first screen on their own:

  • Colouring, Sukhi Colouring, with forty pictures to colour or a blank page
  • Keyboard, Magic Smash by Evandro Meneses, where pressing any key makes something happen
  • First Games, Little Explorer by pazarman, with counting, colours, shapes and animals and no reading needed

All three live inside the download, so they work offline and have no adverts. The tiles are big, bright and marked with simple shapes, so your child does not need to read to choose one. What your child sees shows the rest.

Which sites should I keep off at first?

Anything that asks more of a small child than tapping:

  • Games that need reading to get past a menu or understand the rules.
  • Autoplay video. youtube.com is deliberately not in the catalogue for this reason.
  • Sites with adverts. The catalogue labels each site no ads, ads filtered or ads showing, so you can choose.
  • Logins, chat and purchases. None of these belong on a toddler’s screen.
  • Anything fast, loud or timed. Those tend to make the stop harder.

Should I sit with them the first time?

Yes, for the first few sessions. Show them the tiles, and the big blue Back button at the top of every site that takes them home. Let them press the small ✕ and see that nothing happens without a grown-up.

You will also learn which sites actually work for your child. One that looked lovely on the list may start with a menu they cannot read.

How long should play time be?

Short, to begin with. On the grown-up screen’s Play time tab, pick a length once and it applies every time the app opens. There are shortcuts from ten minutes to an hour, or you can type any number up to four hours.

Your child sees no countdown until the last thirty seconds. Screen time for a three-year-old has what the WHO and the American Academy of Pediatrics suggest, and play time has how it works.

How do I make the stop screen feel normal?

Let an early session end on it while you are there. It says “That is all for now”, and nothing else will open.

Your child can press Ask for more time, but only you can answer, with fifteen minutes, an hour or the rest of the day, for that session only. If you do not want to give more, there is a button for a grown-up to close Sukhi Play from the same screen. It works best when the answer is usually no. The stop is then just what happens, not the start of a discussion.

What if my child learns the grown-up hold?

They might. A toddler does not manage a three-second hold by accident, but one who uses the app every day learns it eventually, and mine did.

When that happens, open Settings and switch Getting in here from Hold to Sum. You are then asked a small addition instead, and holding the button stops doing anything at all. Three wrong answers in a row lock the gate for ten seconds. The grown-up gate has the rest.

How do I add sites as they grow?

Open Grown-ups and hold for three seconds, then:

  • From the catalogue: tap a site and it arrives on the tile screen with everything it needs already filled in. The same list is on the catalogue page.
  • Any other site: type its address. Sukhi Play opens it once, watches what it loads, and works out what to allow. Give the tile a name, a shape and a colour, and press Save.

Each app has a switch on its card, so you can take a tile away for a while without deleting it. Adding and removing sites covers it all.

Jazz’s Studio, the stationery app I made for my daughter, waits in the catalogue too. It is for children of about seven and up, so leave it off for now.

What will it not do?

It is not a substitute for being nearby. The Force Quit keys are left working on purpose so you can always recover the computer, and on Windows the Start menu can appear for a moment before the app closes it. What this cannot do is the honest list, and how to let a toddler use your laptop covers the habits that go with it.

Questions parents ask

Which apps are switched on when Sukhi Play is first installed?

Three that come inside the download: Sukhi Colouring, a keyboard playground called Magic Smash, and a set of first games called Little Explorer. They are files on your own computer, so they work without internet.

Can I hide a tile without deleting it?

Yes. On the grown-up screen each app has a switch in the corner of its card. Turn it off and the tile leaves your child's screen, with everything about it kept for later.

What happens if the tile screen is empty?

It says so plainly and offers a button that takes you to the grown-up screen, where you can switch an app on or add one.

Can I start the setup again from scratch?

Yes. Reset Sukhi Play to factory settings, on the Settings tab, restarts into the walkthrough. It wipes every app and setting, asks twice, and cannot be undone.
