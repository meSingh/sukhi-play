# Parental controls or a locked-down browser for an under-five?

> Parental controls filter what an older child finds online. An under-five needs containment: one screen, only the sites you chose, and no way out.

By Mandeep Singh, 2026-09-26. For parents. https://sukhiplay.com/blog/parental-controls-vs-kiosk-browser/

Parental controls and content filters are built to keep an older child away from bad content while they browse on their own. An under-five has a different problem: they cannot read, they tap everything, and one wrong tap takes them off the page. What they need is containment, a single screen with only the things you chose and no way out, which is what a locked-down (kiosk) browser does.

The two are not rivals. They solve different problems, and plenty of families end up using both.

## What do parental controls actually do?

Most parental controls are a mix of four things:

- **Content filters.** Block categories of website, such as adult content or gambling, and turn on safe search.
- **Time limits.** A daily allowance, or hours when the device is off.
- **Approvals.** A grown-up says yes before an app is installed or something is bought.
- **Reports.** What was used, and for how long.

Good ones are built into everything already: Screen Time on Apple devices, Microsoft's family settings on Windows, Google Family Link on Android, and filtering in many home routers and broadband packages.

They share an assumption: the child is using the device the way a grown-up does. They open a browser, go places, search. The filter's job is to catch the bad places. Everything else is allowed.

## Why does a toddler need something different?

Because a three-year-old is not browsing. They are pressing things to see what happens.

- **They cannot read**, so an advert and a game's play button look the same.
- **They tap everything**, including "skip", "install", "open in app" and the tiny cross that is really part of the advert.
- **They escape the page**, with the back button, a closed window or a palm on the keyboard.
- **Autoplay decides what comes next.** On video sites, the next thing is chosen for them.
- **Adverts appear on perfectly respectable sites**, and a content filter has no reason to block a respectable site.

None of this is inappropriate content, so a filter lets all of it through. The toddler still ends up somewhere they cannot get back from, upset, or on your email.

## What is a kiosk browser?

A browser that starts from "nothing" instead of "everything". It covers the screen, has no address bar or tabs, shows only what you listed, and needs a grown-up to close.

In Sukhi Play that means six layers, described in full in [how the lockdown works](/docs/lockdown/):

1. each site may only load from the hosts you allowed, so ad networks nobody has heard of are blocked simply because they are not on the list
2. a blocklist of known ad and tracker domains, checked even on allowed sites
3. no popups or new windows
4. no navigating to anywhere off the list
5. keyboard shortcuts taken away while it is in front, while game keys still work
6. no downloads, no right-click, and its own browser profile, separate from yours

## How do parental controls and a kiosk browser compare?

| | Parental controls | Kiosk browser |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Starts from | Everything allowed, known bad blocked | Nothing allowed, your choices let in |
| Built for | Children who read and browse | Children who cannot read yet |
| Adverts | Usually not their job | Filtered, and cannot lead off the page |
| Leaving the page | Allowed | Refused |
| Shortcuts and desktop | Untouched | Taken away while it is in front |
| Time limits | Usually per day | Sukhi Play: per session |
| Grows with the child | Yes | Outgrown once they read |

## When is each the right choice?

A kiosk browser fits when:

- your child is under five or so, and does not read yet
- they use two or three sites, not the whole web
- the computer is a shared laptop with your work on it

Parental controls fit when:

- your child reads and searches for things on purpose
- they have their own device or account
- you want reports, or limits across every app

In between, there are good built-in options. On an iPad, Guided Access locks the screen to one app. For videos, YouTube Kids is a much better fit than YouTube, which Sukhi Play deliberately leaves out of its catalogue.

## Can I use both together?

Yes, and for a small child on a shared computer it is the strongest setup:

1. **A separate account for your child,** so your files and logins are not behind whatever they are doing.
2. **Parental controls on that account,** for time limits and anything outside the browser.
3. **A kiosk browser as the thing they use,** so they stay on the screen you chose.

The Sukhi Play docs recommend a separate user account, or Windows Assigned Access, for anyone who wants a guarantee rather than a very good default.

## What does a kiosk browser not do?

It is only fair to say. Sukhi Play does not judge whether a site you add is suitable; it only makes sure nothing else loads. It does not keep a daily total or send you reports. The Force Quit keys are left working so the machine can always be recovered, and on Windows the Start menu can flash up before the app closes it. [What this cannot do](/docs/limits/) lists the rest.

And neither kind of tool replaces a grown-up in the room. They reduce what a small child can reach. They do not childproof a computer.

## Questions parents ask

### Do I still need parental controls if I use a kiosk browser?

Not for what happens inside it, because only the sites you added can load. They are still worth having on the child's account for everything outside it, such as other apps and the time spent on the computer overall.

### Is a kiosk browser suitable for an older child?

It is safe, but it quickly becomes too small. Once a child reads and wants to search for things, parental controls and a conversation about what they find do more good than a screen of six tiles.

### Does Sukhi Play block adverts?

Yes. Advert and tracker requests are cut before they load on every site you add. A few sites ask in their terms not to be filtered, and you can leave those alone; your child still cannot leave the site.

### Can a toddler get out of a kiosk browser?

Not by tapping or by the usual shortcuts. The Force Quit keys are left working on purpose so a grown-up can always recover the computer, and on Windows the Start menu can appear for a moment before it is closed.
