# Free for families, never for sale: Sukhi Play's new licence

> From today, Sukhi Play is free for individuals and families to use, change and share, and never for commercial use or for organisations.

By Mandeep Singh, 2026-09-23. Announcements. https://sukhiplay.com/blog/new-licence/

The short version:

- Sukhi Play stays free for individuals and families, to use, change and share.
- It can't be sold, wrapped in adverts, or used by organisations, schools and nurseries included.
- Versions up to 2.0.2 were released under MIT, and those stay MIT.
- Every line of the code stays public.

I built Sukhi Play for my son, and I give it away so other families can have
the same thing. Until today it was under MIT, a generous licence that lets
anyone do nearly anything with the code. That includes selling it, putting
adverts around it, or folding it into a product, and those are exactly the
things Sukhi Play exists to keep away from small children.

So from today, Sukhi Play and Sukhi Colouring are under a licence of their own:
the Sukhi Play Personal Use Licence.

## What it means for your family

Nothing changes. Sukhi Play is still free, with no account and no paid
version. You can set it up for your children, change it to suit your family,
and give copies to other families, as long as you don't charge for them. Every
line of the code is still public, including the parts that decide what your
child can reach.

## What it rules out

- **Anything commercial**: selling it, charging for it, adverts in or around
  it, collecting data through it, or using it to promote a product.
- **Organisations**: companies, schools, nurseries, childcare providers,
  charities and clubs. Sukhi Play was made for a household, a grown-up and their
  own child, and that's the setting it's for.
- **Hosting it for others**: running it, or something built from it, for people
  outside your own household.

The names Sukhi Play and Sukhi Colouring, and the Sukhi character artwork,
were never part of the offer and still aren't.

## Where it comes from

I didn't write a licence from nothing. It's adapted from PolyForm
Noncommercial, a short standard licence drafted by lawyers, narrowed so it
covers personal use by a household and leaves organisations out.

Everything released before today, up to and including 2.0.2, was MIT and stays
MIT. That can't be taken back, and it shouldn't be.

## Want to help?

Please do. Improvements come back through the project, so every family gets
them. The short terms are in CONTRIBUTING: you keep the copyright in what you
write, and I can use it in Sukhi Play.

This is a plain summary, not legal advice. The full text is on
[the licence page](/licence/), with three plain lines above it.

## Questions parents ask

### Can I still use Sukhi Play at home for free?

Yes. Using it for your own children, changing it for your family and sharing it with other families free of charge are all allowed, and there is still no account and nothing to pay.

### Can my child's school or nursery use Sukhi Play?

No. The licence does not cover use by any organisation, including schools, nurseries, childcare providers and charities.

### What about the version I already downloaded?

If it is 2.0.2 or earlier, it was released under the MIT licence and stays that way. The new terms apply from 2.1 on.
