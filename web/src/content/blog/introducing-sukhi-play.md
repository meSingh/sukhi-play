My son is three, and he loves playing games on my laptop. He also clicks every
button on the screen, which means adverts, popups, new tabs, and eventually my
work. So every time he played, I sat beside him and watched his hands instead
of the game.

I wanted something simple. The few sites I had chosen, as big buttons he could
press without reading, and nothing else. Something I could hand over and
actually walk away from. That is Sukhi Play, and today it's ready for other
families too.

## A laptop is built for grown-ups

Every key on a laptop does something, and a small child doesn't press one key
at a time. They press a handful at once with a flat hand, which is exactly how
the shortcuts that close windows and switch apps are made. Adverts are drawn
to look like play buttons. A video that ends rolls into another one you never
picked. And whatever you were working on is always one stray click away.

None of that is the child's fault. They are pressing things, which is what
three-year-olds are for. The laptop just wasn't made for them.

## What Sukhi Play does about it

It fills the whole screen with a few big, colourful tiles. Each one is a
website you chose, drawn as a colour and a simple shape, because a child who
can't read can still remember that their game is the blue one. They press a
tile, the site opens full screen, and a big Back button takes them home again.

Everything else is switched off. Links to other sites go nowhere, popups and
new windows never open, and downloads are refused. The keyboard shortcuts that
close windows or switch to another app stop working while Sukhi Play is in
front. Adverts are blocked as well, and the bar at the top quietly counts how
many it caught.

So your child can press every key and click every button, and the worst that
happens is they end up back on their tiles.

## One rule for grown-ups

To leave, hold a button for three seconds.

![Sukhi Play asking a grown-up to hold a button for three seconds before it closes](/screenshots/05-closing-needs-a-grownup.png "Closing asks for a three-second hold. A small child's taps are always quick, so they never get through.")

A small child's taps are always quick, so they never get through by accident.
You hold, and you are back in charge: close Sukhi Play, or change what's on the
tiles.

## Setting it up takes a minute

The first time it opens, Sukhi Play asks who's at the computer. A grown-up gets
a short walkthrough: choose the first sites, practise the hold once so you
know how it feels, and hand it over.

Adding any other site is just as easy. Type its address, and Sukhi Play opens
it out of sight, watches what it needs to work, and sets it up for you. You
never need to know what a hostname is.

## Why it's called Sukhi Play

It's named after my son, Sukhi, and the little character on the icon is him.
I built it for him first. If it helps your family too, that's the best thing
that could happen to it.

It's free on Macs, Windows and Linux, with no account and nothing to sign up
for, and all of the code is public for anyone who wants to check how it works.
