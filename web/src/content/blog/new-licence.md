From version 2.1, Sukhi Play and Sukhi Colouring are released under the Sukhi Play Personal Use Licence instead of MIT. They are free for individuals and families to use, change and share, and never for commercial use or for any organisation, schools and nurseries included. Everything up to and including 2.0.2 was released under MIT and stays under it.

## What changes for a family using it at home?

Nothing. Sukhi Play is still free, with no account and no paid version. You can set it up for your own children, change it to suit your family, study how it works, and give copies to other families, as long as you do not charge for them.

The code is all still public. Every line, including the parts that decide what your child can reach, is there to read.

## What is no longer allowed?

Under the new terms, none of these is permitted, whether or not anyone pays:

- **Anything commercial.** Selling it, charging for access to it, showing adverts in or around it, collecting data through it, or using it in, with or to promote a product or service.
- **Use by an organisation.** That includes companies, schools, nurseries, childcare providers, charities, clubs, religious organisations and government bodies, and anyone acting on behalf of one.
- **Hosting it for other people.** Making it, or anything based on it, available over a network to anyone outside your own household or family.

The names Sukhi Play and Sukhi Colouring, the logo and the Sukhi character artwork are not licensed at all. The character artwork was never covered by MIT either; it was carved out from the first release.

## Why change it?

Sukhi Play is one parent's project. I built it for my son, and I give it away so other families can have the same thing.

MIT is a generous licence, and it is the right one for a lot of software. It also lets anyone take the whole thing, put adverts around it, sell it, or fold it into a product. Those are the very things Sukhi Play exists to keep away from small children, and I would rather it could not be turned into one of them.

Organisations are left out for a simpler reason. Sukhi Play was made for a household, a grown-up and their own child, and that is the only setting I am making it for.

It also means the site no longer calls Sukhi Play "open source". A licence that rules out commercial use does not meet that definition, and I would rather not use the words loosely. What stays true is that every line of code is public.

## What happens to older versions?

Versions up to and including 2.0.2 were released under MIT. That cannot be taken back. If you have one of those, it stays under MIT, and everywhere the licence is mentioned on this site says so.

## Where do the terms come from?

I did not write a licence from nothing. The terms are adapted from PolyForm Noncommercial 1.0.0, a short standard licence drafted by lawyers. It allows charities, schools and government bodies, which is not what I want here, so the permitted purpose is narrowed to personal use by an individual for their own household, with organisations named as excluded and hosting for anyone outside the household excluded too. PolyForm's own terms allow their text to be adapted on condition that their name comes out of it, which is why it does not appear in the licence itself.

## What if I want to contribute?

Please do. Improvements reach everyone by coming back to the project rather than living in separate copies. The short terms are in CONTRIBUTING: a contribution comes with a permanent licence for me to use and relicense it, and you keep the copyright in what you wrote. If you would rather not agree to that, say so before you send anything.
